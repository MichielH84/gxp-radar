EU Commission Digital & Data Integrity

EudraLex Vol. 4: Annex 11 (Computerised Systems), new Annex 22 (AI) and Chapter 4 (Documentation) — final texts still pending

Status check, 3 Sep 2026 (drafts 7 Jul 2025; consultation closed 7 Oct 2025)

The three linked EudraLex Volume 4 documents remain the biggest open item on the European GxP calendar. Annex 11 grows from 5 to roughly 19 pages across 17 sections, adding lifecycle validation, supplier oversight, identity and access management, audit trail review and — for the first time — cybersecurity as an explicit GMP requirement. New Annex 22 covers AI/ML in GMP: model selection, training, validation and performance monitoring, restricted to static deterministic models, with adaptive and generative models excluded from GMP-critical decisions. Revised Chapter 4 puts data governance and risk management at the centre of documentation, paper and electronic alike. Final publication has been signalled for 2026 but was not yet in the Official Journal as of this scan.

This is the single strongest door-opener in the European market right now. Every client running MES, LIMS, ERP, chromatography data systems or eBR faces a re-baselining exercise the moment the final text lands. Sellable work: Annex 11 gap assessments against the draft, CSV-to-CSA transition roadmaps, audit trail review programmes, supplier/cloud qualification frameworks, cybersecurity-in-GMP assessments, and an AI inventory plus Annex 22 readiness review. Position now, before the final text drops — clients who wait will need it fast and will pay for speed.

EMA Lab Equipment Qualification & Validation

Concept paper on the targeted revision of EU GMP Annex 15 (Qualification and Validation)

Published 19 Jan 2026 — consultation 9 Feb to 9 Apr 2026; first draft expected end 2026

The EMA GMP/GDP Inspectors Working Group and PIC/S published a joint concept paper for a targeted revision of Annex 15. The headline change: application to active substance manufacturers becomes mandatory rather than optional, extending qualification and validation expectations to chemical and biological API sites. The revision also aligns Annex 15 with ICH Q9(R1) quality risk management. The driver is explicitly the sartan/nitrosamine 'lessons learnt' report, which found weaknesses in process and product knowledge, validation practice, change control and contamination control at API manufacturers. A first draft is planned for the end of 2026.

BD angle

API and intermediate manufacturers — a large slice of the Belgian and wider European chemical-pharma base — have historically run lighter qualification systems than finished-dose sites. They now have roughly 18 months to close that gap. Offer: readiness assessments against Annex 15 for API sites, requalification and periodic review programmes, cleaning validation and lifecycle process validation build-outs, and IQ/OQ/PQ documentation remediation. Pair with ICH Q9(R1) risk-management training to make it a programme rather than a one-off audit.

FDA GxP & Quality Systems

Draft guidance: Container Closure Systems for Human Drugs and Biological Products

Announced 14 Aug 2026 (CDER/CBER/OCP, August 2026)

FDA announced a new draft guidance on container closure systems that, once final, will supersede the 1999 guidance 'Container Closure Systems for Packaging Human Drugs and Biologics' and the related 2002 Q&A document. It modernises expectations for CCS selection, qualification, extractables and leachables strategy, and the supporting CMC documentation for drugs and biologics, including parenteral presentations.

Consulting angles: CCS qualification strategy, extractables/leachables study design and gap review, comparability packages for packaging changes, and CMC dossier updates. Naturally bundles with analytical method work and stability programmes — a good cross-sell into QC labs.

FDA Digital & Data Integrity

Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software — final guidance, February 2026 revision now in force

Final 24 Sep 2025; updated version issued 3 Feb 2026 (QMSR alignment)

FDA's CSA guidance replaces exhaustive test-script validation with a risk-based, intended-use-driven assurance model for software used in medical device production and the quality system. The February 2026 revision aligns the text with the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR), effective 2 February 2026, which incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference into 21 CFR 820. Higher process risk warrants rigorous testing; lower risk can be covered with leaner, unscripted methods and lighter records.

BD angle

Medtech clients now face two simultaneous shifts — QMSR transition and CSA adoption — and most have not rewritten their validation SOPs for either. Offer a combined package: CSA/QMSR gap assessment, rewrite of the validation master plan and software risk framework, retrospective rationalisation of legacy validation packages, and training for QA and IT. The value story is easy to sell: measurably less documentation effort per system.

ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice — Annex 2 adopted and published; consolidated guideline finalised

Consolidated guideline June 2026; Annex 2 adopted July 2026

The E6(R3) modernisation of GCP is now complete with the adoption of Annex 2, which covers non-traditional trial designs — decentralised elements, real-world data sources and digital health technologies. The consolidated Step 4 guideline was finalised in June 2026. E6(R3) embeds quality-by-design, proportionate risk-based monitoring and ICH Q9/Q10-style quality management into clinical trial conduct, with substantially strengthened expectations around data governance and the reliability of electronic clinical data.

Offer: E6(R3) gap assessments, SOP rewriting, risk-based monitoring design, vendor oversight frameworks, and — the crossover with Annex 11 — validation of DCT platforms, ePRO and eConsent systems. USP Lab Equipment Qualification & Validation

Proposed revision of USP <1058>, renamed 'Analytical Instrument and System Qualification'

Proposed in Pharmacopeial Forum 51(2); comment period closed 31 May 2025 — revision pending

The proposed revision reframes AIQ as AISQ — an assurance journey across the full operational life of an instrument or system, from specification and qualification through to retirement, rather than a discrete qualification event. Notably, the provision on combining qualification documents present in the current chapter (and echoed in Annex 15 clause 2.5) does not appear in the updated draft, which has practical consequences for how QC labs structure IQ/OQ/PQ documentation.

BD angle

Combined with the Annex 15 revision, this makes 2026-2027 a genuine refresh cycle for laboratory qualification. Offer QC labs: an instrument inventory and risk classification, redesign of the IQ/OQ/PQ documentation set, periodic review and requalification programmes, and vendor qualification-package assessment. This is repeatable, scalable delivery work — ideal for placing a team rather than a single consultant.

Annex 11 / Annex 22 — the clock is running

Annex 11 is going from 5 pages to roughly 19. Seventeen sections. Cybersecurity written into GMP for the first time. And alongside it, Annex 22 — the first European GMP text dedicated to AI in manufacturing.

The drafts have been public since July 2025. The final texts are expected this year.

Here is what I keep hearing from quality leads: "we will look at it when it is final."

That is understandable, and it is also the expensive option. The gap assessment does not change much between draft and final. What changes is how much time you have to close the gaps you find — and how many consultants are still available when everyone starts at once.

If your MES, LIMS or CDS landscape has not been mapped against the draft yet, that is a two-week exercise today and a six-month scramble later.

Annex 15 revision — API manufacturers, this one is for you

A detail in the Annex 15 concept paper that deserves more attention than it is getting:

Qualification and validation are becoming mandatory for active substance manufacturers. Until now, Annex 15 was optional for them.

The driver is not abstract. It traces straight back to the sartan and nitrosamine investigations, where the lessons-learnt report found gaps in process knowledge, validation practice, change control and contamination control at API sites.

Consultation closed in April. A first draft is expected by the end of the year.

For finished-dose manufacturers this is a modest update. For API and intermediate producers it can mean building a qualification lifecycle from a much lighter starting point — cleaning validation, requalification, periodic review, the full set.

If you make active substances in Europe, the honest question is: would your current validation file survive an inspector reading it against Annex 15 rather than against ICH Q7?

Data integrity: still the same findings, ten years on

From a recent FDA warning letter:

An instrument password written on a sticker, stuck to the laptop.
Analytical data automatically deleted when the laptop shut down.
No record that the quality unit ever reviewed it.

We have been talking about ALCOA+ since 2016. Ten years of guidance, training and conference tracks. And the findings still look like this.

My read is that it is rarely a knowledge problem. People know the principle. What is missing is the boring layer underneath: who owns the audit trail review, how access is provisioned and removed, whether anyone has actually tested that the backup restores.

Those are not glamorous projects. They are also the ones that keep a site off the import alert list.

A one-day walkthrough of a QC lab usually finds three of these within the first hour. It is worth the day.
